Writing messy code, especially when classes become poorly structured and tightly connected, can introduce serious bugs. In real-world applications, bugs are not only technical problems; they can also lead to financial losses. That is why we should write code that is clean, easy to understand, and easy to maintain. Think of a house where electricity wires, internet cables, and other connections are all tangled together. If one wire develops a fault, finding and replacing the correct wire becomes very difficult because everything is tightly connected. The same thing happens in programming when classes are tightly coupled. Fixing bugs, adding new features, or changing one part of the application can become difficult and may even break other parts. To reduce these problems and make applications easier to develop and maintain, we use **design principles**. These principles help us build cleaner, more organized, and maintainable software.
